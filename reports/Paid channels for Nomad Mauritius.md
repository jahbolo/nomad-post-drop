# Keep Facebook, fix the funnel, test Search

Yes. Facebook is the right place for Nomad's small paid budget in Mauritius, and nothing in the research argues for moving it. Facebook reaches about **886,000 people, or 85.5% of Mauritian adults**, more than twice Instagram's reach, and no other platform comes close to it for homeowners aged 35 to 64 ([DataReportal 2026](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-mauritius)). Nomad's own result of about **$0.60 per WhatsApp conversation** is the best benchmark available for this market, and it is well below the only published vendor range of €1.50 to €8.00 per conversation ([Spur](https://www.spurnow.com/en/blogs/click-to-whatsapp-ads-benchmarks)). The weak point is not the channel. It is what happens after the chat opens: nobody yet knows what share of conversations send a bill, get quoted and sign. The recommended plan is to keep Meta as the main paid channel, merge the separate boosts into one always-on Ads Manager campaign, measure bills received rather than chats, run a small Google Search test only if Keyword Planner shows real demand, and keep TikTok, YouTube and LinkedIn organic for now. Two fact-sheet problems need fixing before more ads run: the grant may not apply to every system, and the playbook names two different inverters.

## Facebook reaches most adults; nothing else comes close

The platform numbers settle the main question. Facebook's ad reach of 886,000 compares with **418,000 for Instagram** (40% of adults) and 599,000 for Messenger ([DataReportal 2026](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-mauritius)). NapoleonCat's raw Meta figures are higher, 1.19 million for Facebook, but they include diaspora and duplicate accounts, so treat 0.9 to 1.2 million as the range ([NapoleonCat](https://napoleoncat.com/stats/social-media-users-in-mauritius/2025)). Applying NapoleonCat's age shares to the conservative figure gives roughly **300,000 Facebook users aged 35 to 54**. A $3 a day budget cannot come close to exhausting that pool, so the limits on Meta are creative fatigue and frequency, not audience size.

The gender split is worth noticing. Facebook in Mauritius is about **52% male and 46% female** ([DataReportal 2026](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-mauritius)), so Nomad's heavily male 35 to 54 audience does not come from the platform. It comes from who responds to technical solar content and from Meta's delivery chasing early responders. Women who share the household decision are under-reached. That is a creative question (a finance or bill post framed for the household, for example) more than a targeting one.

Google is the only other channel with clear intent behind it. It holds **91% of search** in Mauritius ([StatCounter](https://gs.statcounter.com/search-engine-market-share/all/mauritius)), and the two 2026 schemes, the CEB household scheme on 11 April and the Rs 75,000 grant on 25 July, should have driven searches ([CEB](https://ceb.mu/news/2026-04-11/launching-of-ceb-solar-photovoltaic-scheme-households-2026); [L'Express via AllAfrica](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202607270203.html)). Nobody publishes Mauritian search volumes or click costs, so the size of that demand is unknown until Javed checks it himself.

|---|---|---|---|

## Nomad's $0.60 per chat is cheap, but chats are not customers

Nomad's numbers are healthy on the surface. About one conversation per 115 to 135 viewers works out to roughly $4.50 to $5.20 per 1,000 people reached. The current boosts sit a little above that: "Is solar worth it?" has spent $5.25 for 730 viewers and 5 conversations, so $1.05 each and one per 146 viewers, and "One leaf" has spent $1.30 for 220 viewers and one conversation. Both are early and too small to judge, but neither is a warning sign on its own.

The risk is treating cheap chats as success. US solar data, all of it directional agency figures, shows Facebook lead-form leads cost about a third of Google leads but close at **3 to 6% against 8 to 15% for Google**, so cost per sale ends up similar or worse on Facebook ([LeadGen Economy](https://www.leadgen-economy.com/blog/google-vs-facebook-solar-leads-comparison/)). Meta's own published A/B tests point the same way: advertisers who stopped optimising for conversations and optimised for leads or purchases cut cost per result by **12 to 79%**, and in-chat qualification cut cost per qualified lead by 31 to 37% ([Spur summarising WhatsApp Business case studies](https://www.spurnow.com/en/blogs/click-to-whatsapp-ads-benchmarks)). In one Indian message-ad case, only **22 to 37% of chats were qualified** ([Spur](https://www.spurnow.com/en/blogs/click-to-whatsapp-ads-benchmarks)). That is not solar data, but it shows the scale of drop-off to expect.

Nomad already has the right qualifier: the bill photo. A chat that sends a bill is a lead; a chat that says "how much?" and disappears is not. The WhatsApp Business greeting and quick replies should ask for the bill straight away, and the lead sheet should track each enquiry as conversation, bill received, quote sent, site visit, signed, with the source recorded. Two months of that will give the first real Mauritian funnel numbers. The arithmetic is still encouraging: at $0.60 a chat, even 2% of chats becoming installs means about **$30 of ad spend per install**, against US acquisition costs of $2,000 to $4,000 per sale ([Rework](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/solar-home-energy-growth/solar-referral-programs)).

## One evergreen campaign beats ten separate boosts

How the money is spent on Meta matters more than which platform gets it. Meta says an ad set needs about **50 optimisation events a week** to leave the learning phase, and that ad sets below it cost about 28% more per result, though it gave no method for that figure ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/meta-says-ad-sets-under-50-weekly-conversions-cost-28-more-per-purchase/)). At $3 a day and $0.60 a chat, Nomad gets about 35 conversations a week, so it will always be "learning limited". That is normal at this spend and not worth fixing with money. What makes it worse is running about ten separate boosts, because each one starts learning from zero and splits the budget.

The better setup is a single Click-to-WhatsApp campaign in Ads Manager, one ad set, with the best-performing posts added as ads by their post IDs, and left alone for at least 7 to 14 days between changes. On audience, one independent 30-day test found **Advantage+ audience beat detailed targeting and lookalikes** on both cost and lead quality ([Jon Loomer](https://www.jonloomer.com/test-results-advantage-plus-audience-detailed-targeting-lookalikes/)). It was a US test, but the logic holds more strongly in a small market, where tight age and interest filters shrink an already modest pool. Both current boosts have Advantage+ off. Turning it on, with Mauritius as the location and a broad adult age range, is the cleanest test available. It is also a likely fix for "Is solar worth it?" spending only $1.25 of its $3 in the last 24 hours, since underspend usually means Meta cannot find enough eligible delivery in the audience it was given. That cause is an inference, not something the notes confirm. Meta also found that ads with more than one message destination had a **9.5% lower median cost per message** ([Spur citing Meta](https://www.spurnow.com/en/blogs/click-to-whatsapp-ads-benchmarks)), but adding Messenger and Instagram DMs splits conversations away from WhatsApp, where the bill and quote happen. Keep WhatsApp only.

## Google is the only real second channel; the rest can wait

Google Search is the one channel with a plausible case for a share of the budget. US solar clicks cost $3 to $18 ([LeadGen Economy](https://www.leadgen-economy.com/blog/google-vs-facebook-solar-leads-comparison/)), but Mauritian auctions likely have only a handful of bidders, so costs should be far lower. That is unmeasured. Two free checks come first: Keyword Planner (Mauritius, English and French terms) for volume and top-of-page bids, and the Google Ads Transparency Center to see which competitors bid. If monthly volume is in the hundreds, a 4 to 6 week test at about $5 a day with exact and phrase match, strong negatives ("kit", "Alibaba", "DIY", "job") and WhatsApp clicks as the conversion is worth running. If volume is tiny, skip it. Local Services Ads are not offered in Mauritius; Google lists only Western European countries and the US and Canada ([Google Ads Help](https://support.google.com/adwords/answer/6224841)).

The Google Business Profile is the highest-return Google asset and costs nothing. In BrightLocal's 2026 survey, **68% of consumers use only businesses rated 4 stars or more**, and 74% look for reviews from the last three months ([BrightLocal](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/)). The playbook's review-at-every-install rule is the right move.

TikTok, YouTube and LinkedIn should stay organic. No public source sizes TikTok or YouTube in Mauritius, and it is not confirmed that TikTok Ads Manager delivers there at all. General TikTok figures show click-through falling from 1.48% at ages 18 to 24 to **0.52% at 45 to 54** ([Web Tonic](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/solar-energy-tiktok-ads-statistics)), the wrong direction for homeowners. YouTube views are cheap, at about $0.05 per in-stream view globally ([Store Growers](https://www.storegrowers.com/youtube-ads-benchmarks/)), but they build familiarity, not WhatsApp messages. LinkedIn's Mauritian membership skews young, with 49% aged 25 to 34 and only **2.1% aged 55 and over** ([NapoleonCat](https://napoleoncat.com/stats/social-media-users-in-mauritius/2025)). It suits businesses and banks from the Proof phase on, not homeowners.

Offline channels are real but belong later. Radio and TV reach **94 to 96% of Mauritians weekly** ([Afrobarometer](https://www.afrobarometer.org/publication/ad877-mauritians-approach-election-with-diminished-confidence-in-the-freedom-of-their-press/)), and a small station like OUI Radio sells 30-second spots for Rs 180 to 225 ([OUI Radio 2026 rate card](https://www.ouiradio.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Grille-Tarifaire-Jan-a-Dec-2026.pdf)), but radio cannot target the 10 to 15 km around recent installs. The home fair, Le Grand salon de la maison et du jardin, ran at SVICC from 22 to 26 April 2026 with about 150 exhibitors ([L'Express via AllAfrica](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202604240218.html)). If it repeats in April 2027 it falls in the Proof phase, when Nomad will have register sheets to show. The partnership worth pursuing now is a bank: MCB's Green Loan offers up to 100% financing over up to 15 years and needs a supplier quote ([MCB](https://mcb.mu/personal/loans/green-loan)), and MCB already lists solar firms as merchant partners. Those listings publish package prices and discounts, for example Leal Energie's 5 kW with 16 kWh at Rs 520,000 ([MCB](https://mcb.mu/personal/offers-and-promotions/detail/Home/leal-energie)), which breaks Nomad's rules. Any Nomad listing would need a different format, such as "MCB green loan accepted, application prepared in-house", with no price. Referrals remain the strongest channel of all: referred solar leads close at about **29 to 30%**, several times any paid lead ([Rework](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/solar-home-energy-growth/solar-referral-programs)).

## Three things to check before the next boost

**The grant may not cover every system.** L'Express reports the Household Rooftop Solar PV Grant covers a **hybrid system of up to 5 kW, or 3.5 kW without a battery** ([L'Express via AllAfrica, 26.07.2026](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202607270203.html)). The CEB connection scheme itself allows up to 10 kWac and has required a battery since 15 April 2026 ([CEB leaflet](https://ceb.mu/files/files/application%20schemes/Information%20Leaflet%20on%20CEB%20Solar%20Photovoltiac%20Scheme%20(Households)%202026.pdf)). If the grant's 5 kW limit holds, Nomad's 10 kW system may get no grant, or only partial grant. That conflicts with the fact sheet's "Grant: 25% of the system, up to Rs 75,000, on every system". Confirm with the CEB (querydcpvscheme@ceb.mu) and update the fact sheet before any ad or post repeats "on every system". Note also that export above 5 kWac needs three-phase under the CEB scheme.

**The playbook names two inverters.** The strengths table in section 2 says "DEYE inverter", while the fact sheet says "SOLIS hybrid · 5 years warranty". The fact sheet is the only source posts may use, so one of these is wrong. Fix it before the next "Inside the system" post or ad.

**The paid-spend rules need a decision.** The playbook says no paid media until shipment 1 is installed, yet boosts are already running, and it pauses spend whenever the backlog passes 4 weeks. If all 8 systems in shipment 1 are spoken for at about 4 installs a month, the backlog is already about 8 weeks. Spending is still defensible if the boosts are explicitly building the shipment 2 waitlist, but Javed should make that call deliberately and tell chats the honest lead time. Meanwhile the creative stays within the rules: no discounts, no urgency, no prices, and every ad ends on sending a photo of the CEB bill on WhatsApp.

## Conclusion

The question was which platform, but the research points to a different constraint. At $0.60 a chat and about 150 chats a month, Facebook can already produce more conversations than an installer doing about 4 installs a month can turn into jobs. Growth over the next six months will come from converting chats into bills and bills into signed installs, then into reviews and referrals, not from finding a cheaper channel. The money Nomad spends on Meta is buying evidence as much as leads: two months of lead-sheet data by source will tell Javed more about Mauritius than any benchmark in this report, because none of the published benchmarks are Mauritian.

One gap matters more than the rest. The grant terms on 10 kW systems are unclear. If the 5 kW cap is real, the honest-price message changes for larger households, and so does the pitch for every ad aimed at high bills. Sort that out with the CEB first.
