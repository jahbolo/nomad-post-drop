# Residential solar lead generation on small paid budgets: channel benchmarks, click-to-WhatsApp, and low-budget Meta practice

Research date: 28 Nov 2026. Most solar-specific channel figures come from US agency and vendor blogs. Many are uncited ranges or self-labelled "planning assumptions". The only primary data sources found were Wood Mackenzie and Meta's own published tests. The reliability of each figure is flagged inline.

Nomad context (given by the coordinator, not researched): about $0.60 per WhatsApp conversation, about 1 conversation per 115 to 135 viewers on $3/day boosts, audience mostly men aged 35 to 54.

## 1. Cost per lead and lead-to-sale conversion by channel

### Takeaway
US benchmarks put Facebook/Instagram lead-form CPL at roughly $20 to $100, against $80 to $300 for Google Search. Facebook leads close far less often, about 3 to 6% against 8 to 15% for Google, which makes cost per sale broadly similar or worse on Facebook. Referrals close at about 29 to 37%, several times any paid channel. No reliable solar-specific CPL or close-rate data was found for emerging or island markets, only agency estimates.

### Cited Findings
**US macro customer acquisition cost (primary source)**
- US residential solar CAC was $0.60/W in 2025, a five-year low and 10% below 2024. Wood Mackenzie forecasts $0.84/W in 2026, a 40% rise, after the Section 25D 30% tax credit expired at the end of 2025. It also expects the residential market to contract 19%. Published 25 Mar 2026. — [Wood Mackenzie](https://woodmac.com/news/opinion/us-residential-solar-customer-acquisition-costs-set-to-spike-40-in-2026-before-gradual-decline/)
- Applying those $/W figures to a 7 kW system gives about $4,690 (2024), $4,200 (2025) and $5,880 (2026, forecast) per customer. This is a vendor blog's arithmetic, not observed data. — [SurgePV](https://www.surgepv.com/blog/solar-customer-acquisition-cost)
- CAC is about 30% of revenue per sale for national players and about 15% for local installers. Bodhi Solar analysis, undated vendor figure, cited via SurgePV. — [SurgePV](https://www.surgepv.com/blog/solar-customer-acquisition-cost)
- Industry CAC is $2,000 to $4,000 per sale and cost per referral customer is about $500. Attributed to NREL community research via Aurora Solar, cited secondhand. — [Rework](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/solar-home-energy-growth/solar-referral-programs)

**US CPL and close rates by channel (agency figures: directional, mostly unsourced)**
- Google Ads CPL: $80 to $150 for exclusive basic leads, $110 to $220 for form-verified exclusive leads, $45 to $85 for shared leads, $180 to $280 for set appointments. — [LeadGen Economy, Nov 2025, updated Mar 2026; reflects late 2024 to 2025](https://www.leadgen-economy.com/blog/google-vs-facebook-solar-leads-comparison/)
- Facebook CPL: $25 to $55 for native lead forms, $35 to $65 for higher-intent forms, $45 to $90 via a landing page, $20 to $40 for retargeting. — [LeadGen Economy](https://www.leadgen-economy.com/blog/google-vs-facebook-solar-leads-comparison/)
- Lead to appointment: Google 25 to 35%, Facebook lead ads 10 to 20%, Facebook landing page 18 to 28%. Lead to sale: Google 8 to 15%, Facebook lead ads 3 to 6%, Facebook landing page 5 to 9%. In the worked example, cost per sale is about $1,300 on Google and $2,500 on Facebook lead ads. The article calls these "directional guidance" and ties them to no source. — [LeadGen Economy](https://www.leadgen-economy.com/blog/google-vs-facebook-solar-leads-comparison/)
- Rocket Launch Media figures (undated), cited via SurgePV:

  | Channel | CPL | Lead to sale |
  |---|---|---|
  | SEO / inbound web | ~$196 | 10 to 20% |
  | Google Ads | $100 to $300 | 5 to 15% |
  | Purchased leads | $25 to $150+ | 5 to 8% |
  | Facebook / Instagram | $20 to $100 (often $30 to $50) | not published |
  | Booked appointments | $200 to $500 | not published |
  | Referrals | not published | ~29 to 37% |

  — [SurgePV](https://www.surgepv.com/blog/solar-customer-acquisition-cost)
- Shared leads, sold to 2 or 3 installers, close at 3 to 5%. Exclusive qualified leads close at 8 to 15%. Lead fraud in third-party lead buying is estimated at 25 to 35% (industry estimate). — [LeadGen Economy, cited via SurgePV](https://www.surgepv.com/blog/solar-customer-acquisition-cost)
- Close rates by channel: social and aggregator leads 5 to 10%, referrals 29.2%, Google Local Services Ads inbound phone 17%, Google residential 15%, Solarize community campaigns 33%. Phone leads convert at 46% against 1.7% for form leads. All unattributed. — [Web Tonic, Aug/Oct 2026](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/solar-energy-tiktok-ads-statistics)
- No verified benchmarks were found for door-to-door, telemarketing or events. — [SurgePV](https://www.surgepv.com/blog/solar-customer-acquisition-cost)

**Emerging markets (estimates only)**
- India: Facebook CPL of ₹200 to 600 (₹200 to 500 for lead-gen campaigns, ₹100 to 300 for retargeting). Close rate 8 to 18% (8 to 15% lead-gen, 20 to 35% retargeting), halving to about 6% with slow follow-up. ₹15,000 to 25,000 a month yields 40 to 80 leads for a small installer (EPC). The author says "every cost, close-rate and budget figure here is a planning assumption, not a measured benchmark." Updated 31 Oct 2026. — [QuickEstimate](https://quickestimate.co/blog/facebook-ads-for-solar-india)
- South Africa: residential PV cost per qualified lead of R450 to R900, C&I R1,800 to R3,500. Google yields 8 to 15 qualified residential leads per R10,000. A budget of R30,000 to 65,000 a month brings 40 to 80 qualified leads. A "representative" installer case improved quote-to-close from 11% to 23% and cut survey-to-quote time from 9 days to 2.5 days using WhatsApp template follow-up among other changes. All unsourced or illustrative. Published May 2026, updated Sep 2026. — [Growth Pulse Media](https://www.growthpulsemedia.co.za/b2b-lead-generation-for-solar-renewable-energy-south-africa/)

### Inferences
- Across the US sources, Facebook's cheap CPL is offset by a close rate one-half to one-third of Google's. The metric to manage is cost per signed install, not cost per lead or conversation.
- Speed of follow-up shows up repeatedly as a large driver of close rate (India: 12% vs 6%; South Africa: faster quotes in the case example). This matches Nomad's same-working-day WhatsApp reply rule.
- Nomad's ~$0.60 per conversation is far below any US CPL. A conversation is a much weaker signal than a form lead, though, so the two numbers are not comparable.

### Gaps
- No primary, solar-specific CPL or close-rate data was found for Mauritius, the Caribbean, Kenya, Nigeria, the Philippines or Pakistan. Searches returned only agency marketing pages.
- No reliable data was found for door-to-door or events CPL or close rates.
- d.light, SolarAfrica and similar companies (mostly PAYG or C&I models) were not researched in depth because of the search budget.

## 2. Click-to-WhatsApp ("Get more messages") performance and best practice

### Takeaway
No independent public benchmark exists for cost per WhatsApp conversation. Meta's own A/B case studies show that optimising for leads or purchases instead of conversations cut cost per lead or purchase by 12 to 79%, and that in-chat qualification forms (WhatsApp Flows) cut cost per qualified lead by 31 to 37%. The consistent lesson is that cheap chats are not the goal. Judge results on qualified leads and qualify inside the chat.

### Cited Findings
- No independent public benchmarks exist for click-to-WhatsApp CTR, cost per conversation or conversion rate. — [Spur, retrieved Sep/Oct 2026](https://www.spurnow.com/en/blogs/click-to-whatsapp-ads-benchmarks)
- Meta WhatsApp Business success stories, A/B tests switching from conversation optimisation to lead or purchase optimisation:
  - Braip (Brazil): 30% lower cost per lead.
  - Nutryfit (India and Gulf, A/B test 1 to 18 Apr 2025): 12% lower cost per lead.
  - Confer On (Indonesia): 79% lower cost per purchase.
  - Thành Vinh (Malaysia): 40% lower cost per purchase.
  - VIP Leather Craft (Indonesia): 21% lower cost per purchase.

  — [Spur summarising WhatsApp Business success stories](https://www.spurnow.com/en/blogs/click-to-whatsapp-ads-benchmarks); see also [WhatsApp Business: Nutryfit](https://whatsappbusiness.com/resources/success-stories/nutryfit/)
- WhatsApp Flows (in-chat forms):
  - Learnwise (India): 31% lower cost per qualified lead.
  - Engelife (Brazil): 37% lower cost per qualified lead and a 26-point higher qualification rate.
  - Arabian Automobiles (UAE): 56% lower cost per lead.

  Meta marks all three as self-reported. — [Spur](https://www.spurnow.com/en/blogs/click-to-whatsapp-ads-benchmarks)
- Meta experiment across 734,000 businesses (three weeks, H2 2024): ads with more than one message destination (for example WhatsApp plus Messenger and Instagram) had a 9.5% lower median cost per message than WhatsApp-only ads. — [Spur citing Meta's click-to-message ads page](https://www.spurnow.com/en/blogs/click-to-whatsapp-ads-benchmarks)
- House Hunt case (India, Instagram DM ads, not WhatsApp): after adding chat qualification, cost per chat rose from ~₹33 to ~₹36. Cost per qualified lead fell from ₹150 to ₹98, and the qualified share of chats rose from 22.1% to 36.9%. — [Spur](https://www.spurnow.com/en/blogs/click-to-whatsapp-ads-benchmarks)
- Meta's "messaging conversations started" metric counts new contacts, or contacts returning after 7 days of inactivity. Chats from click-to-WhatsApp ads open a free entry window that Meta's docs now say "may remain open for up to 7 days" (previously 72 hours) on mobile, with a reply required within 24 hours. — [Spur citing Meta docs, 28 Sep 2026 changelog](https://www.spurnow.com/en/blogs/click-to-whatsapp-ads-benchmarks)
- Recommended practice:
  - Measure on qualified leads and revenue, not chats.
  - Send lead and sale events back to Meta through the Conversions API for Business Messaging.
  - Measure revenue over 30+ days.
  - Run A/B tests for at least 7 days, preferably 1 to 2 weeks.
  - Batch-test 3 to 5 creatives with a visible WhatsApp cue.

  — [Spur](https://www.spurnow.com/en/blogs/click-to-whatsapp-ads-benchmarks)

### Inferences
- For Nomad, a qualified lead is a bill photo received. If the WhatsApp Business app (not the API) is used, quick replies and a greeting that asks for the bill photo do the job of a simple qualification step. Flows require the API.
- At about 1 conversation per 115 to 135 viewers, Nomad's chat rate is healthy. The open question is what share of chats send a bill. The House Hunt numbers suggest 20 to 40% is a plausible qualified share for message ads in general, but that is not solar data.
- Lead-optimised messaging campaigns need conversion events sent back to Meta. Without the API or CAPI, Nomad is limited to conversation optimisation.

### Gaps
- No solar-specific click-to-WhatsApp case study with cost per conversation was found.
- No country benchmark for cost per conversation in Mauritius or Sub-Saharan Africa was found.

## 3. Boosted posts vs Ads Manager; learning phase at about $3/day; Advantage+ audience vs manual targeting

### Takeaway
Evidence that Ads Manager beats boosting is old and click-based. One 2020 test found Ads Manager gave 18% lower CPC and 64% more reach. Meta still describes about 50 optimisation events per 7 days as the threshold to exit the learning phase, and says ad sets below it cost about 28% more per result. That 28% figure is unverified. At $3/day an ad set cannot reach 50 events a week. In one independent test, Advantage+ audience beat detailed targeting and lookalikes.

### Cited Findings
- Agorapulse test (published 3 Aug 2020): $750 each on boosted posts and Ads Manager ads. Ads Manager produced 855 clicks against 729, reach of 66,284 against 40,354, and CPC of $0.93 against $1.14. Boosted posts had the higher CTR (1.81% against 1.29%). The test is confounded: boosted posts used automatic placements while Ads Manager ran on desktop feed only. — [Agorapulse](https://agorapulse.com/social-media-lab/facebook-boost)
- Meta, 21 Oct 2026: "Roughly fifty conversions a week. Below that the system is guessing." Meta says such ad sets "ran about 28% more expensive per purchase", but gives no methodology. Significant edits restart learning. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/meta-says-ad-sets-under-50-weekly-conversions-cost-28-more-per-purchase/)
- Vendor reports say the threshold fell to 10 events for some campaign types in 2024. Meta's documentation kept 50 events in 7 days. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/meta-says-ad-sets-under-50-weekly-conversions-cost-28-more-per-purchase/)
- Jon Loomer saw a 10-events-in-3-days learning phase that later reverted to 50 in 7 days, with no explanation, probably a test. — [Jon Loomer](https://www.jonloomer.com/qvt/learning-phase-10-or-50-events/)
- Jon Loomer A/B test: 30 days at $25/day per ad set, optimised for registrations, in the US, Canada and Australia. Advantage+ audience had the lowest CPM and the most registrations: 14 more than detailed targeting and 43 more than lookalikes in CRM data. It also had the most quality registrants, with detailed targeting 17% lower and lookalikes 54% lower. The gap with lookalikes was statistically significant; Meta gave detailed targeting a 36% chance of winning a rerun. — [Jon Loomer](https://www.jonloomer.com/test-results-advantage-plus-audience-detailed-targeting-lookalikes/)

### Inferences
- At $3/day and about $0.60 per conversation, Nomad gets about 35 conversations a week ($21 / $0.60). That sits below the 50-event threshold, so a single ad set will likely stay in learning, or "learning limited". Practical responses:
  - Run one ad set only.
  - Avoid frequent edits.
  - Pool budget into one evergreen campaign rather than many short boosts.
  - Accept that "learning limited" is normal at this spend.
- Each new boost starts its own learning from zero. Running one Ads Manager campaign with several post creatives (using existing post IDs) keeps learning in one place, so its advantage over boosting is structural, not just click cost.
- Advantage+ audience evidence is from one US test. In a small market like Mauritius, a broad national audience with Advantage+ is probably sensible; heavy age and gender restrictions shrink an already small pool. The men 35 to 54 skew may simply be who Meta finds responsive.

### Gaps
- No controlled test was found comparing boost and Ads Manager on messaging objectives or at very low budgets in 2023 to 2026.
- No published evidence was found on Meta delivery quality at $1 to $5/day specifically.

## 4. TikTok ads for solar and home improvement

### Takeaway
No credible solar-specific TikTok CPL or lead-quality data was found. Available figures are general TikTok benchmarks from an agency blog, mostly unattributed.

### Cited Findings
- TikTok benchmarks:
  - Median CPC $0.62 (cited to adliftr 2026, not solar-specific).
  - CPM $14.80 for lead gen.
  - Median CPA $27.80 for US local services.
  - CTR 1.62% for Spark Ads against 0.84% for in-feed ads.
  - CTR by age falls from 1.48% (18 to 24) to 0.52% (45 to 54). CPA rises from $11.20 (18 to 24) to $58.40 (55+). Unattributed.

  Published Aug 2026, updated Oct 2026; the page shows signs of incomplete editing. — [Web Tonic](https://www.webtonic.io/blog/solar-energy-tiktok-ads-statistics)

### Inferences
- If the age pattern holds, TikTok reaches homeowners aged 35 to 54 less efficiently than Meta. This supports the playbook's use of TikTok organically for reach, with paid spend staying on Meta.
- Spark Ads (boosting an organic post) outperform standard in-feed ads on CTR. If TikTok is ever paid for, boosting Javed's existing explainers is the format with the evidence behind it.

### Gaps
- No solar TikTok case study with CPL or close rate was found, for any geography.

## 5. Referrals and word of mouth

### Takeaway
Referred solar leads close at about 29 to 30%, against single digits for purchased digital leads (Sunvoy data). The claimed referral share of appointments, 20 to 35%, is uncited. US installers typically pay referrers $200 to $500 in cash.

### Cited Findings
- Referral close rate ~29 to 30% against 2 to 5% for purchased digital leads (Sunvoy independent-installer data). — [Rework, Jul 2026](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/solar-home-energy-growth/solar-referral-programs)
- Referrals "can generate 20-35%" of residential solar appointments. Uncited. — [Rework](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/solar-home-energy-growth/solar-referral-programs)
- A "NREL community adoption study" reports that 80% of referral customers refer after install, with a median of 3 referrals per adopter. No link was given, so this is unverified. — [Rework](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/solar-home-energy-growth/solar-referral-programs)
- Referral rewards:
  - $200 to $500 at install is "common", sometimes split between contract and install.
  - Diminishing returns are claimed above $250.
  - Recommended ask moments: post-install onboarding (days 1 to 7), a six-month check-in, and after a positive review.

  All the author's observations. — [Rework](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/solar-home-energy-growth/solar-referral-programs)
- US installers (Allterra Solar, Simpleray, Maine Solar Solutions) all pay cash when a referral closes. They promote the programme in system manuals, newsletters and a customer app, and train installers to raise it. No share-of-sales figure is given. — [Solar Power World, Sep 2020](https://www.solarpowerworldonline.com/2020/09/referrals-are-a-positive-side-effect-of-strong-solar-customer-relationships/)
- Sunvoy claims doubling referral volume nearly halves acquisition cost, with no supporting data. — [SurgePV](https://www.surgepv.com/blog/solar-customer-acquisition-cost)

### Inferences
- The playbook's referrer-only reward and handover-pack ask match US practice. The recommended ask timing (handover, then six months) maps onto Nomad's NS sheet handover and its "six months on NS-001" content.

### Gaps
- No rigorous, cited share of US solar sales from referrals was found. The SolarReviews 2025 installer survey PDF contained no lead-source data.
- No emerging-market data on referral share was found.

## 6. Conversations or leads to site visit to signed install

### Takeaway
US agency figures put lead to appointment at 10 to 35% depending on channel, and lead to sale at 3 to 15% for paid channels and about 30% for referrals. No source measures WhatsApp conversation to site visit for solar.

### Cited Findings
- Lead to appointment: Google 25 to 35%, Facebook lead ads 10 to 20%, Facebook landing page 18 to 28%. Lead to sale: 8 to 15%, 3 to 6% and 5 to 9% respectively. — [LeadGen Economy](https://www.leadgen-economy.com/blog/google-vs-facebook-solar-leads-comparison/)
- India Facebook leads close at 8 to 18%, labelled planning assumptions. — [QuickEstimate](https://quickestimate.co/blog/facebook-ads-for-solar-india)
- South Africa: in a representative, illustrative case, quote-to-close went from 11% to 23%. — [Growth Pulse Media](https://www.growthpulsemedia.co.za/b2b-lead-generation-for-solar-renewable-energy-south-africa/)
- Message-ad chats were 22 to 37% qualified in the House Hunt case (real estate, India, Instagram DMs). — [Spur](https://www.spurnow.com/en/blogs/click-to-whatsapp-ads-benchmarks)

### Inferences
- A rough planning funnel for Nomad, which is an inference, not a benchmark: 100 ad conversations, then perhaps 20 to 40 bills received, then a share of those quoted that accept a site visit, then signed installs in the low single digits up to about 10. Nomad's own lead sheet is the only reliable source. Tracking "conversation, bill received, quote sent, site visit, signed" by source will produce Mauritius-specific numbers within a month or two.
- At $0.60 per conversation, even a 2% conversation-to-install rate would give about $30 of ad spend per install, very low against any US CAC. That supports continued small spend if the installer has capacity, within the playbook's 4-week backlog rule.

### Gaps
- No measured WhatsApp-to-site-visit or WhatsApp-to-install rate was found for solar in any market.
