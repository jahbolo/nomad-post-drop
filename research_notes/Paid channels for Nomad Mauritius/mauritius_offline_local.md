# Offline and local marketing channels in Mauritius for a small residential solar installer

Research date: 28 November 2026. Every figure below comes with a source and its date. Where rates or audience figures are not public, these notes say so.

## Radio in Mauritius: audience shares and ad rates

### Takeaway
Radio and TV together reach nearly everyone in Mauritius every week. However, no recent station-level audience shares could be found for MBC, Radio Plus, Radio One or Top FM, and none of those four publishes a rate card online. The only public 2026 Mauritian radio rate card found is from the small station OUI Radio, where a 30-second spot costs about Rs 180 to 225.

### Cited Findings
- Per Afrobarometer, 94% to 96% of Mauritians use television and radio at least "a few times a week". The page gives TV and radio as one combined range. Social media is at 88%, internet at 79% and newspapers at 46%. Published 9 October 2024; the page does not name the survey round or sample size. — [Afrobarometer AD877](https://www.afrobarometer.org/publication/ad877-mauritians-approach-election-with-diminished-confidence-in-the-freedom-of-their-press/)
- OUI Radio (Oui-Prod Ltd, Vacoas) rate card for January to December 2026, prices in Rs:
  - A 30-second spot costs Rs 178 to 225 depending on the slot. The highest is 18h–21h on weekdays: Rs 215 from January to October, Rs 225 in November and December. Breakfast (06h–09h) is Rs 180 on weekdays.
  - Day packages for 06h–18h: Rs 550 for 4 spots, Rs 1,000 for 6 spots, Rs 1,500 for 12 spots.
  - A last-position spot costs 40% extra, and first position is also sold as an option.
  - Orders must be confirmed 48 hours ahead. The card does not mention VAT.
  — [OUI Radio Grille Tarifaire 2026](https://www.ouiradio.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Grille-Tarifaire-Jan-a-Dec-2026.pdf)
- l'express's group (La Sentinelle) also ran a radio stream, with 129,997 radio listens a month in March 2018. This is old and is online listening only. — [La Sentinelle digital rate card, March 2018](https://www.lasentinelle.mu/sites/default/files/statistics/lsldigital_ratecard_March_18.pdf)

### Inferences
- OUI Radio is a small station. Rates at Radio Plus, Top FM, Radio One or MBC are probably several times higher, but this is not verified. The OUI card shows the *structure* of local pricing: per-spot rates by daypart, cheap day packages, and a year-end premium.
- Because radio reaches nearly everyone every week, it suits awareness. It cannot target by district the way the playbook's "10 to 15 km of recent installs" rule needs. It may fit better later (Scale phase) than now.

### Gaps
- No current (2024–2026) audience-share figures per station for Radio Plus, Top FM, Radio One or MBC were found online. Kantar's Africascope survey covers some francophone African countries; I could not confirm it covers Mauritius.
- No public rate cards for Radio Plus (Defimedia group), Top FM, Radio One (Sentinelle group) or MBC. Rates must be requested from each station's sales team.

## Print and online news: reach and ad rates

### Takeaway
About half of Mauritians read newspapers at least a few times a week (2024). The only public price list found for a major title is old: l'express's 2018 digital rate card, where one sponsored article cost Rs 30,000 excluding VAT. No current rates are public for l'express, Le Defi Media, Le Mauricien or newsmoris.

### Cited Findings
- 46% of Mauritians use newspapers at least a few times a week, against 88% for social media (published October 2024). — [Afrobarometer AD877](https://www.afrobarometer.org/publication/ad877-mauritians-approach-election-with-diminished-confidence-in-the-freedom-of-their-press/)
- lexpress.mu digital rate card, March 2018, excluding 15% VAT:
  - One native/sponsored article costs Rs 30,000, and a series of four costs Rs 105,600. Each is promoted for at least one week.
  - A homepage takeover costs from Rs 12,000 a day, or Rs 60,000 a week.
  - A newsletter logo and link costs Rs 3,000 a week, or Rs 10,560 a month.
  - Mobile app sponsorship costs Rs 15,000 a month.
  - Reported traffic: 1.2 million unique users a month, 175,471 visits a day, 67% of visits on mobile, 28% international, 21,529 daily newsletter subscribers.
  — [La Sentinelle LSL Digital rate card, March 2018](https://www.lasentinelle.mu/sites/default/files/statistics/lsldigital_ratecard_March_18.pdf)

### Inferences
- Treat the 2018 Rs 30,000 sponsored-article price as a floor indication only; 2026 rates are likely higher.
- A sponsored explainer (for example, how the 2026 CEB grant works, written by an engineer) fits Nomad's "teach, don't sell" voice better than display banners.

### Gaps
- Current rate cards for l'express, Le Defi Media (defimedia.info, Le Defi Quotidien), Le Mauricien and newsmoris are not published online. Print readership per title (2024–2026) was not found.

## Billboards and outdoor ad costs

### Takeaway
Media owners do not publish billboard rental prices. The public cost is the government fee: the MRA charges an annual fee per structure, from Rs 2,500 for 2 m² or less up to Rs 35,000 for more than 36 m² (2026). A new digital network, Alliance Media's Konekt, launched in July 2026 with 60 LED screens at mall and supermarket entrances. Its prices are not public.

### Cited Findings
- MRA Advertising Structure Fee for 2026, per structure per year:
  - Rs 2,500 for 2 m² or less.
  - Rs 1,250 more for each extra m², up to Rs 15,000 at 12 m².
  - Rs 20,000 for 12–24 m², Rs 25,000 for 24–33 m², Rs 30,000 for 33–36 m², Rs 35,000 above 36 m².
  - Due by 2 February 2026, or in quarterly instalments. Late payment costs a 5% penalty plus 1% a month.
  — [MRA notice, Advertising Structure Fee 2026](https://www.mra.mu/download/AdvStrucFee231225.pdf)
- Alliance Media Mauritius launched "Konekt" on 30 July 2026, after a Rs 25 million investment:
  - 60 LED screens at entrances to more than 20 commercial sites, including all Ascencia malls, Tribeca, ten Winners supermarkets and several independent malls.
  - The company claims over 1 million weekly passages, about 15 million impressions a month, and reach to about 95% of people who shop at modern retail.
  - Screens run 08:00–19:00. No pricing was published.
  — [allAfrica / l'express, 6 Aug 2026](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202608060585.html)
- Metro Express sells advertising on its trams (LRVs) and at stations, with no public prices. — [Metro Express advertising page](https://mauritiusmetroexpress.mu/advertising/?lang=en)
- Times OOH runs digital advertising at SSR International Airport. — [Moodie Davitt Report](https://moodiedavittreport.com/?p=429786)

### Inferences
- The MRA fee falls on whoever owns the structure. It is a small part of what an advertiser pays to rent a billboard and is not a rental price.
- Konekt screens at Winners and mall entrances are an alternative to a roadside billboard, though they cannot target an area finely. The playbook books a billboard only in Aug–Sep 2027, from revenue, so quotes can wait until then.

### Gaps
- No public rental rates for roadside billboards (for example 4×3 m or 12×3 m panels) from any Mauritian operator. Quotes must be requested.

### Takeaway
The main consumer home fair is "Le Grand salon de la maison et du jardin", run by Publi Promo Ltée. Its 2026 edition was its 40th, held at SVICC from 22 to 26 April with about 150 exhibitors. If it keeps that timing, the next edition would fall around April 2027, but that is not confirmed. Mauritius Clean Energy Week (8 July 2026, Hennessy Park Hotel) looks like a business and policy conference, not a homeowner fair. Stand prices are not public for either.

### Cited Findings

  - Opened 22 April 2026 for five days, 10:00–19:00, open until 21:00 on Friday and Saturday.

- Mauritius Clean Energy Week 2026, 4th edition: 8 July 2026 at Hennessy Park Hotel. MCCI took part. The page states no organiser, audience or exhibitor costs. — [MCCI](https://www.mcci.org/en/media-news-events/business-updates/the-mcci-at-the-mauritius-clean-energy-week-2026/)
- The 2025 edition was covered by SolarQuarter, which suggests an industry and investor audience. — [SolarQuarter, Aug 2025](https://solarquarter.com/2025/08/19/mauritius-clean-energy-week-2025-wraps-up-with-purposeful-impact-fueling-the-islands-sustainable-energy-journey/)

### Inferences

### Gaps

## Partnerships: banks, government rooftop schemes and installer listing

### Takeaway
Two 2026 state schemes now shape the market. The first is the CEB Solar PV Scheme (Households) 2026, opened 15 April 2026: a battery is compulsory, the limit is 10 kWac, and households choose net or gross metering. The second is the Household Rooftop Solar PV Grant, launched 25 July 2026: 25% of the system cost, up to Rs 75,000, with DBM loans for the balance. No official register of approved installers was found; the CEB only requires a "qualified installer" to sign the Certificate of Installation. Banks reach solar buyers through green loans and merchant-offer listings. MCB already lists at least two solar firms as partner merchants.

### Cited Findings
**CEB Solar PV Scheme (Households) 2026**
- Eligibility and system rules:
  - Open to CEB domestic customers only.
  - From 15 April 2026, only hybrid systems (PV plus battery) qualify, and the battery must cover at least 3 hours of evening demand.
  - The maximum is 10 kWac. Export is capped at 50% of capacity, and export above 5 kWac needs three-phase.
  - The application costs Rs 2,000, non-refundable. Connection and meter fees are extra.
- Tariffs: gross metering is credited at Rs 4.20/kWh. Under net metering, excess energy is credited at Rs 3.00/kWh, with Tariff 150A_NM for most homes.
- Installer role: a "qualified Installer" certifies the Certificate of Installation, the as-made schematic and the battery details. The leaflet names no registered installer list, accreditation, or IEC requirement, only the Grid Code.
- Finance: green loans from commercial banks, DBM concessionary loans, and a Solar Energy Investment Allowance that individuals can deduct on their income tax return.
— [CEB information leaflet, Solar PV Scheme (Households) 2026](https://ceb.mu/files/files/application%20schemes/Information%20Leaflet%20on%20CEB%20Solar%20Photovoltiac%20Scheme%20(Households)%202026.pdf); [CEB communiqué, 11 Apr 2026](https://ceb.mu/news/2026-04-11/launching-of-ceb-solar-photovoltaic-scheme-households-2026)

**Household Rooftop Solar PV Grant Scheme**
- Approved by the Council of Ministers on 24 July 2026 and launched 25 July 2026 by the CEB, with Minister Patrick Assirvaden.
- Grant: 25% of the system cost, up to Rs 75,000 per household.
- Budget: Rs 1.2 billion over 3 years, starting with Rs 270 million from the Climate Sustainability Fund.
- Target: 14,500 households over three years, 3,600 in year one.
- Systems covered: hybrid up to 5 kW, or 3.5 kW without a battery.
- The Development Bank of Mauritius (DBM) lends the balance after the grant.
- The article does not mention installers.
— [allAfrica / l'express, 27 Jul 2026](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202607270203.html)
- **Conflict:** the grant article says households can choose a hybrid system up to 5 kW *or 3.5 kW without a battery*. The April 2026 CEB leaflet says new applications without a battery are no longer accepted and allows up to 10 kWac. The grant may therefore have its own size limits, separate from the connection scheme's. This needs checking with the CEB.

**Banks**
- **MCB Green Loan (personal):**
  - Covers PV panels, solar water heaters and rainwater harvesting, including installation.
  - Up to 100% financing, repaid over up to 10 or 15 years, subject to approval. Rates are not stated.
  - Applicants need a supplier quotation.
  - Applying unlocks "offers from participating suppliers".
  — [MCB Green Loan](https://mcb.mu/personal/loans/green-loan)
- MCB's "offers and promotions" section lists solar firms as merchant partners:
  - **Leal Energie** offers 5% off public prices, or fixed packages: 3.5 kW with 10 kWh at Rs 275,000, 5 kW with 16 kWh at Rs 520,000, 10 kW with 32 kWh at Rs 750,000. Valid to 30 June 2027 for MCB Home Loan cardholders.
  - **CleanPower Solutions** has a similar listing.
  - The pages do not explain how a supplier becomes a partner.
  — [MCB offer: Leal Energie](https://mcb.mu/personal/offers-and-promotions/detail/Home/leal-energie); [MCB offer: CleanPower Solutions](https://mcb.mu/personal/offers-and-promotions/detail/Home/cleanpower-solutions)
- MCB SME Sustainable Loan, "energy transition and efficiency" category, for business systems. — [MCB SME sustainable loan](https://mcb.mu/sme/borrow/sustainable-loan/sustainable-loan/energy-transition-efficiency)
- MCCI publishes a photovoltaic fact sheet for business members. — [MCCI PV fact sheet](https://www.mcci.org/media/139135/photovoltaic-fact-sheet.pdf)

**National context**
- The US Commercial Guide (energy section, February 2026) cites a Renewable Energy Strategic Plan 2025–2030 target of "300,000 systems installed by 2027" for rooftop solar. It gives no residential installer count or market size. — [trade.gov, Mauritius Energy, 19 Feb 2026](https://trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/mauritius-energy)
- Earlier government programme: the CEB Home Solar Project gave rooftop systems to about 2,000 low-income households. This was a state-run programme, not an installer channel. — [IRENA, Jan 2024](https://www.irena.org/News/articles/2024/Jan/Home-Solar-Project-Reduces-2000-Mauritian-Households-Electricity-Costs-and-Carbon-Emissions)

### Inferences
- In practice, "getting listed" means two things: being able to sign the CEB Certificate of Installation as a qualified installer, and becoming a bank merchant partner. The MCB partner route is the most visible: the bank's own green-loan customers see the partner's offer.
- MCB partner listings put package prices or discounts on a bank page. That clashes with Nomad's rules against published prices and discounts. A partnership would need a different format, such as "MCB green loan accepted, application prepared in-house" with no published price.
- The 2026 grant comes with a target of 14,500 households and state messaging. That will push up the number of homeowners searching and asking around, which favours installers who explain the grant simply.

### Gaps
- No evidence was found of an official CEB, MARENA or EEMO register of approved residential installers, or how to join one. Ask the CEB at querydcpvscheme@ceb.mu.
- How a supplier joins MCB's merchant-offers programme is not public.
- SBM, AfrAsia and the DBM loan terms for solar were not checked in detail; no pages were fetched.
- Whether the 25% grant applies to 10 kW systems is unclear (see the conflict above). This matters for Nomad's fact-sheet line "Grant on every system".

## How Mauritian solar and home-improvement companies market themselves

### Takeaway
The visible evidence is thin. Residential solar firms show up through bank merchant-offer pages with published package prices (Leal Energie, CleanPower Solutions on MCB), the home salon, and the press. Utility and commercial players (GreenYellow, Qair, IBL Energy, Omnicane, Alteo and others) appear in trade and business media and at Clean Energy Week. No systematic evidence was found on which residential installers use radio, billboards or Google Ads.

### Cited Findings
- MCB merchant offers publish residential solar package prices and discounts (Leal Energie, CleanPower Solutions). — [MCB Leal Energie offer](https://mcb.mu/personal/offers-and-promotions/detail/Home/leal-energie); [MCB CleanPower Solutions offer](https://mcb.mu/personal/offers-and-promotions/detail/Home/cleanpower-solutions)
- Utility and commercial players named in the US guide: GreenYellow, Qair, Cluny Solar, IBL Energy, Terragen, Omnicane, SeaBrew Solar, Alteo. — [trade.gov, Feb 2026](https://trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/mauritius-energy)
- Directories list Mauritian installers: ENF Solar and Expat.com. — [ENF Solar Mauritius installers](https://www.enfsolar.com/directory/installer/Mauritius); [Expat.com photovoltaic directory](https://www.expat.com/en/business/africa/mauritius/33_energy/photovoltaic/)
- Konekt's launch article says advertisers previously had to deal with several separate outdoor operators and ran "traditional PVC panel campaigns". This is a general statement, not specific to solar. — [allAfrica, Aug 2026](https://fr.allafrica.com/stories/202608060585.html)

### Inferences
- Publishing package prices and discounts appears common among residential competitors. That supports the playbook's view of the category as "loud" on price. Nomad's no-published-price, quote-from-your-bill position stands out against it.
- Free listings (ENF Solar, Expat.com, goafricaonline) are a low-effort way to be found.

### Gaps
- No source showed which residential solar installers advertise on Radio Plus or Top FM, on billboards, or through Google Ads. This needs manual checks: the Meta Ad Library, Google Ads Transparency Center, listening to the stations, and a drive along the M1 and M2.
- No data on how many residential installers exhibited at the 2026 salon.

## Word of mouth and community in Mauritian purchase decisions

### Takeaway
No Mauritius-specific survey on word of mouth, village councils or religious and social groups in solar or home-improvement buying was found. What was found is indirect: very high weekly use of social media and broadcast media, and community-level state solar projects such as Henrietta and Résidence Muguet that put systems on many neighbouring roofs at once.

### Cited Findings
- 88% of Mauritians use social media at least a few times a week (2024). — [Afrobarometer AD877](https://www.afrobarometer.org/publication/ad877-mauritians-approach-election-with-diminished-confidence-in-the-freedom-of-their-press/)
- UNDP/GCF deployed 400 rooftop residential PV systems in one locality, Henrietta. — [UNDP](https://www.undp.org/mauritius-seychelles/news/advancing-low-carbon-transition-through-deployment-400-rooftop-residential-solar-pv-systems-henrietta)
- UNDP/GCF gave free rooftop PV kits to 46 households at Résidence Muguet, Montagne Longue. — [UNDP](https://www.undp.org/node/399331)

### Inferences
- State programmes have rolled solar out by neighbourhood. That fits the playbook's assumption that neighbours seeing an install is a main driver, but there is no direct evidence on how people decide.
- Village councils, municipal councils, temples, mosques, churches and sociocultural associations are plausible venues for a calm grant-and-finance talk. This is untested; no source confirms how well they work for solar.

### Gaps
- No Mauritian consumer research on referral share or trust sources for home-improvement purchases was found.
- The rules and costs for presenting at village council or religious and social group meetings were not found.
